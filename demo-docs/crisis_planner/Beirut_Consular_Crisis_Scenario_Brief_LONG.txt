Title: Consular Crisis Scenario Brief — Beirut Mission (High-Risk Civil Unrest)
Prepared for: Consular Crisis Response Planner pilot simulation

---

1. Scenario Overview
At 06:40 local time, protests in central and southern Beirut escalated into widespread civil unrest following contested political developments and fuel-price shock protests. Multiple arterial routes have intermittent blockades. Security incidents are concentrated in mixed commercial-residential corridors and near transportation nodes.

The Bangladesh mission is receiving fragmented distress calls from migrant workers, students, and short-term visitors. Several callers report inability to move due to roadblocks, fear of opportunistic violence, and intermittent mobile network service.

---

2. Mission Context
Mission location: Beirut
Estimated Bangladeshi nationals potentially affected in-country: 50,000 (including registered and unregistered)
Mission immediate response objective: Preserve life, establish accountability, maintain communication, and prepare for phased relocation/evacuation if conditions deteriorate.

Mission staffing snapshot (active at crisis onset):
- Head of Mission: 1
- Deputy Head of Mission: 1
- Consular officers: 3
- Political desk officers: 2
- Security liaison staff: 2
- Admin/logistics staff: 4
- Locally engaged support staff: 6 (availability uncertain by district)

---

3. Resource Inventory (Initial)
Transport assets:
- Mission vehicles: 2 operational, 1 under maintenance
- Partner fleet access: conditional support from contracted logistics provider (subject to movement windows)

Communication assets:
- Hotline: active but congested
- WhatsApp broadcast channel: active
- Email channel: active, delayed delivery spikes
- Backup communication: limited satellite phone access via partner mission (not guaranteed continuous)

Shelter and welfare:
- Temporary shelter partner capacity: nominal 200 persons for short-duration stay
- Emergency relief kits in mission stock: limited (food, water, first aid)
- Medical referral partners: 2 hospitals with uncertain admissions throughput

---

4. Confirmed Constraints
- Telecom outage: intermittent mobile/data collapse across two high-risk districts
- Roadblocks: dynamic barriers on key arterial roads
- Curfew risk: host-country authorities considering nighttime curfew window
- Fuel instability: uncertain refueling availability for continuous convoy movement
- Border route uncertainty: cross-border movement policy may shift with little notice

---

5. Operational Risks
R1. Communication blackout risk:
Inability to reach unregistered nationals may cause undercounting, delayed rescue, and misinformation spread.

R2. Mobility risk:
Roadblocks and sporadic violence may isolate clusters of nationals from mission support corridors.

R3. Capacity mismatch risk:
Potential shelter demand may exceed available mission-partner capacity by a wide margin during peak displacement.

R4. Escalation risk:
Localized unrest could rapidly spread, converting controlled response posture into emergency execution posture.

R5. Staff fatigue and continuity risk:
Sustained 24-hour operations with limited team depth may impair decision quality unless shift controls are imposed.

---

6. Priority Population Segments
P1. Nationals in immediate high-risk zones (injury/active threat exposure)
P2. Women, children, elderly, and medically vulnerable nationals
P3. Nationals with expired/irregular documents (higher movement and detention risk)
P4. Remote workers without reliable communication access
P5. Students in clustered accommodation with limited supplies

---

7. Command and Coordination Expectations
- Mission must run a tiered posture model (Yellow/Orange/Red) with explicit trigger thresholds.
- HQ expects scheduled SITREP cadence with exception reporting for mass-casualty, detention spikes, and route collapse.
- Coordination required with host-country interior/security contacts and nearby partner missions for contingency channels.

---

8. Trigger Conditions (Draft)
Yellow to Orange trigger indicators:
- Verified multi-district disruption > 4 hours
- Hotline distress volume exceeds processing threshold
- Repeated inability to verify national clusters in two or more districts

Orange to Red trigger indicators:
- Confirmed casualty cluster among nationals
- Mission convoy movement blocked across all primary routes
- Telecom collapse persists beyond operational tolerance window

---

9. Required Planner Outputs (for pilot quality check)
- Distinct condition actions (not repeated generic advice)
- Role-specific task assignment using mission command titles
- Four-phase timeline (0-2h, 2-6h, 6-24h, 24-72h)
- Communication templates for nationals, families, employers, and HQ
- Fillable SITREP format with clear placeholders
- Explicit assumptions and unknowns tied to this Beirut scenario

---

10. Planning Notes for Evaluators
This brief intentionally includes realistic operational friction (telecom instability, route uncertainty, constrained shelter capacity, high affected population). High-quality outputs should explicitly account for these constraints and avoid generic evacuation language detached from resource limits.

End of crisis scenario brief.
